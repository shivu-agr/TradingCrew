Horizon evidence standards (generic).

SHORT (up to about 60 trading days)
Needs a catalyst clock (earnings, corporate action, policy effective date)
and a tape that can actually move on that clock. Options crowding and
event proximity are first-class. A multi-year compounder story is not a
short-horizon reason to buy. If the only evidence is a long-term CAGR
line, the short stance should be mixed or sit-out.

MEDIUM (about one quarter to one year)
Needs a path from the next few prints to the thesis: guidance versus
actuals, forward estimates and revisions, peer relative value, and a
macro basket that actually applies to the home country and industry.
A single headline does not set a medium stance.

LONG (one year and beyond; narrative may run to several years)
Needs multi-year cash-flow quality, reinvestment returns, balance-sheet
trajectory, and ownership stability. Policy and industry structure can
dominate. Outcome accounting is still bounded; do not pretend a ten-year
path is already measured. If long-term data is missing, say so and do
not fill the gap with the short-term tape.

Disagreement across tiers is expected. A name can be a short-horizon fade
and a long-horizon compounder, or the reverse. Copying one stance into
all three is a process failure, not conviction.
