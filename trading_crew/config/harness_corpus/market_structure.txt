Market-structure pattern classes (generic; not issuer-specific).

Policy-driven tape
A listed name can reprice when a rule, tariff, export control, subsidy, or
industrial-policy instrument changes the cash it can earn — not because a
headline exists, but because the instrument maps onto that issuer's country,
product, customer, or input. Short-horizon work asks whether the instrument
is already in the last print. Medium-horizon work asks implementation lag
and second-round volume. Long-horizon work asks whether the policy resets
the industry's return on capital.

Related-party and circular capital
Cash that leaves an issuer and returns through affiliates, promoters,
captive customers, or layered vehicles can inflate revenue or support the
share without new economic demand. Evidence is ownership overlap, unusual
receivables, related-party footnotes, and buybacks funded by intra-group
paper. Absence of a filing is not proof of absence; it is a coverage gap.

Narrative versus cash-flow
A popular story (category growth, platform shift, geopolitical winner) can
outrun free cash flow, working-capital drain, or guidance quality. Short
horizon: crowding and options skew. Medium: revision momentum versus
reported cash conversion. Long: whether ROCE and reinvestment rates
support the story after the narrative cools.

Ownership and control webs
Who can force a recap, a related-party deal, or a squeeze matters more than
the headline float. Promoter pledges, state control, dual-class votes,
and concentrated institutional blocks change both governance risk and
the path of a drawdown. Treat an ownership map as a hypothesis to verify
in filings and live search — do not invent a graph.
